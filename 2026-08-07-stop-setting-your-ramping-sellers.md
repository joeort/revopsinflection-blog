Sales quota setting is simple in theory. Take your target, divide it by reps, add planned hires... boom, you're done. Except it never works out that way. Most quota capacity plans are built on fantasy assumptions that collapse the moment reality hits. Cue the Mike Tyson quote about having a plan.

As we enter planning season, it's a good time to check in on why that simplistic approach is setting you up for failure.

## Top Down

Taking the plan and distributing it across teams without any nuance is a surefire way to reduce attainment and increase rep attrition. This is the challenge if CROs aren't present or too new to push back on finance-led numbers.

## 3 Mistakes

  * Fantasy ramp schedules

    * If you're selling large enterprise deals and your ramp schedule is 6-9 months, you're delusional. Review historical actuals where possible and understand a realistic output over time rather than a wishful expectation. If you don't have history to work from, a rule of thumb would be:

      * SMB - 1-6 months (depending on how transactional the sales motion is)

      * MM - 6-12 months (depending on ASP and cycle lengths)

      * ENT - 12+ months (not the 9 months your plan will require)

  * Unrealistic annual performance expectations

    * As you look at your quota attainment, how is it distributed? Do you have 1 person hitting $1M and everyone else is around $600k? If you set everyone's target at $1M, you're continuing your 60% quota attainment expectation (which based on data would actually be higher than most). Set targets that are realistic based on actual production vs a hope and dream.

  * Segment agnostic

    * If you aren't setting different ramp schedules by different segments (product, segment, region, etc) it's likely not going to align with reality. Certain regions may have better brand recognition or more headwinds from competitors and enterprise sellers will naturally have longer cycle times so their ramp will be longer than SMB/MM reps. Be sure to apply the right schedule to the right roles.




If you get this wrong, expect to see increased rep attrition after they realize they can't hit plan and make their OTE.

## A **Tactical Framework (Step-by-Step Diagnostic)**

  1. List out all of your Quota Carrying Reps (QCR)

     1. Include their start dates, roles and any motion differences or regional differences

     2. Check out our template here: https://www.revopsinflection.com/roi-blog/lets-get-tactical-rep-ramp-assumptions

  2. Instead of cohorting by start date, put M+n across the top to review the first 12 months of cumulative productivity

  3. Extract any outliers - founder-led handoffs, inherited deals, etc that will distort real ramp productivity

  4. Determine the average performance by role/motion while looking at any deltas in cohorts. Simplify to practical tiers (e.g., 0%, 25%, 50%) rather than chasing false precision like 6.5% or 11.2%. The denominator is what is realistic productivity and quota for ramping reps.




## Wrapping it up

Use those realistic productivity numbers and ramp schedules will give you a more realistic view into the production you can expect from new hires. If you are planning for a higher number from a ramping rep, you need to document the assumptions/things you must believe to be true. 

Watch out for the cumulative effect of small changes. Just as you can get exponential growth by just getting 1% better every day, a 10% increase in ASP and a 5% lift in win rate and a shortened ramp time by 30% all contribute to exponential increases in production by ramping headcount. 

Before you go deep into planning, be sure to validate your seller ramp assumptions against actual performance. Don't let your plan be distanced from reality and set up your new hires for failure.
