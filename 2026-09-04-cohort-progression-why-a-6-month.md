Whenever we start new projects with clients, we like to get a handle on key metrics such as ASP, cycle length, win rates, etc. The problem with averages like these is that they hide a lot of the nuance and insights about the business.

If you can quickly answer a question like "what's your average sales cycle?" and the answer is 6 months, how many deals close within that 6 month window? How much skew is in that? How does that vary by segment? Trying to plan/model/forecast by averages is simple and easy to understand but what if you could go deeper?

#### Analyzing the Data

This is a tough to read which is why RevOps should build/analyze and condense the takeaways/outcomes for the organization. I call it the spaghetti chart because it looks like it's thrown against the wall to see what sticks.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!ZQYz!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F0ef55f72-9584-46db-b611-461633827f72_913x805.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!ZQYz!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F0ef55f72-9584-46db-b611-461633827f72_913x805.png)

Each line represents a quarter (can be months/years depending on your business) of when something was created. The X-axis is the timeline in days since create. The Y-axis is the % of the created pipe moved to won by that period, cumulatively. It should converge to your overall win rate on a weighted average basis.

Looking more closely, there are multiple stories that can be told from this chart.

  * **Recent deals are moving slower than this time last year:** Historical cohorts (2025) were converting ~2-4x better in create-to-close in the first couple of months after create. I would dig in on many dimensions to try to understand why that is the case (new segments/markets? newer sales reps? competitive pressures?)

  * **After 6 months shows steeply declining likelihoods to win:** You can see a steady progression up to the 6 month mark where the majority of deals have closed. Another view of this is to look at the progression of all won deals as a % of total won (all approaching 100% eventually). That would tell us that we should be very critical of any deals aging beyond that 6 month mark.

  * **Create and close in period reduces the walk-in pipeline required:** Even more enterprise motion organizations typically have some small share of bookings that can be generated within the quarter. Understanding how much typically comes in helps you forecast numbers in period and evaluate what you truly need to have in the quarter when the quarter begins.




#### The Diagnostics - Why This Happens

The data provides the visibility. The power comes from the decisions/actions you can take from that visibility.

  * **Top-of-Funnel Drag:** The initial 30-to-60-day conversion decline in recent 2026 cohorts indicates qualification leaks or poor speed-to-lead execution, allowing bad-fit deals to sit in early stages without progressing.

  * **Forecast Inflation:** Relying on late-stage spikes (like the day 360+ jumps) causes GTM teams to hold onto old pipeline, assuming "it will eventually convert" rather than aggressively purging aged out pipeline. Taking away that inflated pipeline brings your coverage down. It's better to have a realistic view into coverage early so you can identify that risk in advance.

  * **Capacity Misalignment:** If your capacity model assumes reps close X% of created pipeline within 90 days, but your current cohort curves show a drop from 15% down to 4%, you are heading toward a massive quarterly revenue shortfall. There are multiple reasons why the data looks like that: seasonality, prior year anomaly or large deal concentration. Dig in to your data to understand.




#### The Actions You Can Take Today

Build the views and identify what your spaghetti is telling you. If your chart looks like the above,

  1. **Purge Zombie Pipeline After Day 180:** Establish strict CRM stage-gating. If an opportunity hits 180 days without a good reason why it should remain in pipeline, clear it out. Create a new Opp when the buyer re-engages in earnest.

  2. **Re-evaluate Top-of-Funnel Entry Criteria:** Address the 2026 early-velocity drop by tightening MQL-to-SQL handoffs and review qualification criteria for what gets into pipeline.

  3. **Map Sourced-Based Velocity Curves:** Segment this cohort view by deal source (inbound vs outbound vs sales vs partner). Outbound enterprise deals naturally follow a 12-month curve, whereas inbound mid-market should flatten much earlier.




#### The Takeaway

Average cycle times lie... progression curves reveal the real health of your GTM engine. If your early cohort velocity slows down, no amount of late-stage discounting will save the quarter. Get ahead of it by monitoring your curves and identify risks in advance.
